# The Party Behind the Presses

She was looking over the monitors one last time to make sure the plant was humming along when the glowing red lights of the trailer filled the camera. “I’m headed to solids, you got this?”

Todd looked up from the catechisms he was studying and nodded. She walked down the stairs, out the door, and over to the building where the solids were thickened and processed for disposal. The crickets and cicadas were competing to be the loudest. The roar of water falling drowned them out as she approached the building. Martine should be under the presses, washing them down.

She stepped inside and waited a moment for her eyes to adjust to the brightness. She looked around, but didn’t see him. She walked to the center, still not seeing him. She made a right, heading under the presses where she was immediately enveloped in fog. She looked for the flashing light of his personal air monitor, but it was just her. She stopped for a minute listening closely. Again, she heard only the machinery, the roar of water falling, and the voices.

She pauses for a minute and thinks, *Perhaps he’s in the polymer room*. She pivots a little too quickly and slips on the wet floor. She regains her balance and steadies herself against a concrete column. Certain no one had seen her stumble, she starts forward and hears laughter from the voices, as if some speaker had just told a joke. She shrugs it off and enters the polymer room. No Martine in here either. She starts up the two flights of stairs to the office, but pauses on the first landing when her own air monitor starts flashing and beeping.

She looks up at the exhaust fans and sees they are rusted shut again. She makes a mental note to put in a work order for maintenance and crosses to the loading dock door. She hits the button and raises it about a foot before descending the stairs two at a time. She knows the door likes to stick and puts her shoulder into it to shove it open. It is indeed stuck. She shoves a little harder and it opens, right into Martine. Fog and laughter roll slowly across the floor, being pushed by the cross-breeze she had created. She looks up at Martine. “Sorry about that. Are you okay?”

Martine looks more panicked than hurt. “Yeah-yeah. Let’s just get up to the office.”

Martine brushes past her and takes the stairs three at a time to the office. She sticks her head out the door looking to see what spooked Martine, but other than the fog created from the cold fall night and the warm air of the solids and water, nothing seems to be there. She hears the indistinct voices at the edge of her hearing. The voices merge together into an eerie sound reminiscent of a crowded party beyond a closed door. But that was just the sounds of the solids building.

She kicks the wedge into the door, making sure it won’t slam shut from the breeze. She starts up the stairs and is halfway up the first ones when the mixing tank next to her starts and she jumps. Spooky seems to be the theme of the night, despite it being a decade since the plant had scared her. She knows every sound, every smell, and the timing of every piece of automated equipment. That’s what a good operator does, develops the ‘feel’ of the plant.

Martine is a good operator. He had been around the plant almost as long as she had, but had only been a full-time operator for about half of that. She’d never known him to let things like tonight spook him before, other than the cemetery which bordered the north and west side of the plant. She chuckled at that and thought maybe the guys had been telling stories again.

She was given all of the legends and ghost stories when she became an operator. How the old broken wooden train trestle was a train robbery gone bad and the robbers killed everyone. How there was a hell’s gate where the trestle, plant, and cemetery met. How the gate was opened because of the robbery and the fact it was ancient burial ground for the Plains Indians that frequently inhabited the area. None of it spooked her. She had grown up learning the spirit ways and she found a certain peace to cemeteries. She had even joked that the voices were the folks buried in the cemetery having a party and the solids building was the ballroom.

She ascended halfway up the second flight of stairs and paused as the window to the office came into view. Martine is seated at the desk with his head bowed. His forehead rests against his clasped hands. The Bible is open in front of him and from his hands dangles a rosary. Martine is deep into prayer with his eyes closed and she can see the sweat shining off his face. Up until now, she had assumed the Bible and rosary in the desk were Todd’s as he was converting to Catholicism. Never did she imagine they were Martine’s. Martine is truly terrified.

After waiting for Martine to finish his prayer, she climbs the final steps and opens the door. “You okay?”

Martine blushes and replies, “A little better now. I forgot to take the rosary with me when I went to swap the trailers out. Is it always this foggy this late at night?”

“Yes. Sorry, I forgot it’s your first time on the graveyard shift.”

Martine swallows hard enough she sees his Adam’s apple move up and down. “Please don’t say graveyard.”

She looks at the calendar on the wall and notices today is circled with the words ‘Día de los Muertos.’ She chuckles. “Okay. Come on, let’s get the presses shut down so we can do the PM.”

Martine nods and places the rosary in his shirt pocket and rises but pauses at the door once more, listening closely. She places a reassuring hand on his shoulder, and he takes a deep breath before walking out the door. She follows him out and they make quick order of shutting and washing the presses down.

The sudden silence of the machinery causes a slight ringing in her ears. Through that ringing, she still hears the voices. The voices came from somewhere beyond the belt presses. Not words exactly, just conversation. Dozens of overlapping conversations. She looks over at Martine, and his right hand is across his body, clutching the rosary through the fabric of the pocket. His lips are again moving in prayer.

She looks down the stairs and sees the last six steps are hidden in the fog. She touches his arm as she passes by him. “Say one for me too please.”

She moves down the steps watching the fog begin to swirl and the voices start to grow louder. She hears Martine following her and the sound of their boots on the metal of the steps almost sounds like champagne glasses clinking together. As she reaches the steps where the fog is lingering, it starts to part almost as if welcoming her to the party.

She steps cautiously on to the wet cement floor, adjusting her footing not to slip again. She walks around the stairs heading under the presses, still watching the floor and her footing, when suddenly the fog parts completely. The voices grow louder and she looks up. A cold breeze comes from behind her, giving her chills. The hair on her arms starts to rise as the goosebumps form. She is almost afraid to look up and when she finally does shock takes over and she’s frozen in place.

What she sees is the breeze moving and shaping the fog into human forms. A swirl here and a lovely woman with an hourglass shape and long hair holds a champagne flute. Another swirl and a tall man in a tux appears talking animatedly with his hands. The voices grow louder but are still not coherent enough to allow for individual conversations. More swirling of the fog and the people multiply, their conversations become louder. Faintly out of nowhere, a single guitar begins to play.

She half turns to ask Martine if he hears the music too and realizes he has stopped next to her, six steps from the floor. This time he has removed the rosary from his pocket and once again is praying. She knows he heard the music too. She returns her gaze to beneath the presses and the fog has now finished setting the scenes. It had to be close to, if not more than, a hundred people, laughing, talking, having a party. Suddenly they all stop talking at once and turn to look at her, but they really aren’t looking at her, just past her.

They all begin to raise their glasses as if in response to a toast. The voices silent and she risks a look behind her. Martine had finally made it to the floor and was standing behind her. She turns fully to look at him. His face and body are drained of all color. His hand out holding the rosary and cross as a defensive weapon. The rosary begins slightly swaying in the breeze. The music has stopped. The voices have quieted.

Martine raises his hand a little higher brandishing the cross, but this doesn’t have the desired effect. The ghosts in the fog seem to think he is toasting them back and she hears a collective cheer ring out. She turns around and looks at the people who seem to be emptying their glasses as if the toast was final. Their voices start again. The music begins to play, and she watches in fascination as the fog seems to form and reform as the people shift.

She turns back to check on Martine and he’s not there. She scans the area looking for any sign of where he might have gone when she finds him a few feet away. He has fainted from whatever visions he saw. She looks back over her shoulder. The party is back into full swing. She turns back to Martine and is about to key her radio when the fog envelops him. Out from his chest arises the shape of Martine, who glides past her to join the party. She watches as he melds into the sea of people as though he belonged there.

Her imagination thinks the worst. She checks him for a pulse. It is good and strong. She glances once more at the party the fog created, but it seems to have faded. She can no longer make out individual people, just an arm or head here and there. She returns her attention back to Martine. He is waking up and rubbing the back of his head. She offers him a hand up and helps him outside for some fresh air for them both. As she is about to leave the building, she pauses in the doorway watching the fog swirl, still hearing the voices with words still out of reach and thinks, *Maybe I wasn’t joking after all*.
