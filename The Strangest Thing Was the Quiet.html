The Strangest Thing Was the Quiet

I wake and reach for Joey and he’s not there. I don’t know if he’s gone gone or just gone until he works himself out. The ache for him hits me hard because it’s been a long time since he was here. The feeling he isn’t coming back grows stronger every day. I roll over to look at the time, hugging my pillow tight, trying to send the hug to him. I am hoping he can still feel the love I have for him.

I hear Trent’s door open. Mine’s ajar but not closed. Next, I hear him let Bebe out, her nails clicking on the tile floor. She gets in the bed with me, covering me with puppy kisses. I giggle and push her off, deciding I might as well get up. The house should feel empty with Joey gone, but the strangest thing was how comfortable the house felt with Trent in it.

I let her and Ares out into the yard. I arrive in the kitchen where Trent is leaning against the counter watching the Keurig brew and yawning. I slide next to him, put my arm around his waist, give him a hug, and see the Keurig says, ‘Brewing Complete and Enjoy.’ I steal the coffee cup, chuckling low as I go to the fridge for creamer. He says, “Thief!”

I reply while pouring, “That’s what you get for letting the barky-butt in my room.”

He says, “Told ya, shut the door.”

I remind him he knows that doesn’t work because of Tang. On cue, Tang appears, stretching to the top shelf. Tang has to inspect if I choose coconut creamer or milk, his favorite. This morning before I get a chance to answer, Trent says, “No sir. You are getting too fat.”

He makes Tang move so I can close the door. I laugh, sitting at the table across from Trent. We finish breakfast, change into swim wear, and grab our bags. We drive to the gym and stretch before he helps me into the shallow end of the pool. Still feeling mischievous, I dive under and knock his legs out, emerging laughing. He resurfaces sputtering and dunks me under. I rise just in time to hear him challenge, “Race you!”

He wins when I stumble and can’t keep up. We walk the pool for about thirty minutes, laughing and talking. We sit in the jacuzzi for about fifteen minutes, lost in our own thoughts, comfortable in each other’s company. I think about how strange it is that Trent is the one taking care of me after knee surgery instead of Joey. We towel off and return to the house in that same comfortable silence.

He waits until I am stable in the shower before going to the bathroom across the house. I get dressed, and grab my laptop, starting work for the day from the couch. He comes out in a towel dripping. I chastise him for leaving puddles on the floor. He apologizes, blaming the wet-dripping-streak through the house on his deodorant in his bag on the washer. He stops behind me and shakes his head, laughing as I try to protect my laptop. He pads off to the kitchen, and I tell him to stay out of the cookie jar. The chink of the glass lid sounds, and he tosses me two Oreos on his way back to his room.

A few minutes later, he’s dressed and tells me to quit working so hard while he lets the dogs in. I make us protein shakes for lunch and chicken Caesar flatbread sandwiches. I turn around, and Trent is not at the table. Then, I hear the piano. I am getting knocked around by the dogs trying to get me to drop something. I stop and set down the plates, grabbing raw hides from the drawer. They scatter, and I have to remind Bebe to get off the couch as I pass on the way to the music room.

I set the plates and drinks down on the table and sit next to him at the piano. He looks over at me with a questioning look, and I start playing chopsticks. He smiles and joins me in the duet. He hits a wrong note, and I hip bump him. He bumps back. We keep trading bumps until I knock him off. He’s rubbing his backside and asks me to help him up. As I reach for his hand and clasp on, he pulls me down, ending in a pile of arms and legs, giggling.

He reaches up and hands me a plate and glass before taking his own. We eat lunch, talking about my new book and his music. Then he catches Tang trying to steal chicken from his flatbread sandwich. I laugh at the two of them as he gives up dodging paws and teeth, letting him eat the chicken dangling from the sandwich. He gets a call and gathers the dishes, retreating to the kitchen for some privacy. I stretch out on the couch and grab my laptop to work on the book, not letting the ache of missing Joey fill the comfortable silence with Trent.

Thunder crashes, and I switch tabs to check the radar. He quietly comes in, watching me with a hand on the back of the couch. His face close to mine, we study the maps together, seeing there was no need to head down to the storm shelter. I’m about to close my laptop when a reminder pops up for a movie rental that is expiring. He pats my shoulder and starts back toward the music room with Tang swatting at his ankles. I yawn and stretch, heading to the kitchen for another glass of tea, watching Trent try to make it to the piano bench before Tang trips him.

The next couple of hours pass with him playing notes on the piano and scribbling while I peck on the laptop. I finish the chapter and close the laptop. Watching Trent work on his song, trying different keys and rhythms, Joey starts to creep into my thoughts and dissolves just as quickly as it was forming. For a moment, everything feels suspended, the room holding its breath. A whispered thought materializes. Trent and I fell into this comfortable silence when he moved in three months ago. He senses it too, looks up and smiles at me. He rises and plants his elbows on the back of the couch, watching me. “It’s getting late. Better chop my veggies before I get fired from the kitchen because I’m just not making it as a songwriter.”

I throw the pillow I was leaning against at him before following him to the kitchen. We move as a synchronized team preparing dinner. A hand on a shoulder here. A touch of an arm there. A brush of bodies in the small kitchen going from stove to pantry to refrigerator. We eat dinner trading creative suggestions on each other’s projects. We clear our plates and as I lay them in the sink, I look outside and drop them with a loud clank in the sink. I go out the door, and Bebe is there with a branch from my poor lilac bush. He walks up behind me, puts one arm around my waist, and hands me a saw. He pats me on the head and exits with his shovel, replacing the dirt and rocks she had dug up.

We return to the house, and I remember the expiring movie. He makes the popcorn, and I go to the living room, turning the TV on and queuing up the movie. Trent reaches around and hands me the bowl, then jumps over the back of the couch to sit next to me. He puts one arm on the back of the couch, lightly brushing my shoulder. Balancing the bowl between us, we try to keep the dogs and Tang from stealing or spilling the popcorn.

We are enjoying the movie until the intimate scenes on a yacht. All I can see is me and Joey, not the characters. A tear rolls down my cheek. For a moment, the room feels suspended, the movie fading into a blur as the past and present overlap. He knows but doesn’t say anything. He lifts my face to his, using a thumb to wipe the tear away. Then he pulls me close for the hug I didn’t know I needed. We break apart, his arm around my shoulders, and I lean into him, placing my head on his shoulder. He kisses the top of my head. We finish the movie tucked into one another, a perfect fit.

He gets a call from his ex, and I go to my room to give him privacy. I’m getting ready for bed when I knock over my jewelry box. The infinity bracelet Joey gave me for my birthday falls out. I remember that birthday weekend and all that since has gone so wrong. The loneliness and hurt hit, I reach for him, but he’s not here. I lay down crying. I feel Trent slide in behind me, wrap himself around me, and hold me tight. He pulls my hair back and kisses my cheek, whispering, “I know. Just let me hold you.”

I don’t fight him or ask him to leave, because the strange thing is, this comfortable silence between us feels like home. I wake up the next morning, still wrapped in his arms. Two people with a presence inside the silence.
