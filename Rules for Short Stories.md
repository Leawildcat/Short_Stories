# Rules for Short Stories

Editing & Publish Ready

1. Most of the short stories are from prompts given to me by the Bedtime on the Porch group on Substack. The point is that I'm to write them and am often given an extra challenge (pov, genre, person, etc.) by Lisa or Sacred. I welcome prompts and direction from you but not whole written blocks. I have to write them in a short time frame and they are generally read on Saturday nights by myself. I have not decided how I will publish them yet, and for now live in the Github pages and repository for short stories. The general rules for the short stories are as follows:  

- Between 1500 and 2000 words, but I have been known to go over or under depending on the story.
- No intentional harming of animals by humans, but animals can be harmed by other animals or natural causes.
- No child abuse, sexual abuse, or sexual assault.
- Erotica is allowed, but has to use happy bunny and hop hop hop for the sexual acts.

1. NO EM or EN DAHSES, hyphens okay and expected for words and phrases only (numbers are hyphenated because my math brain demands it) New exception to the rule is for cut off dialog ONLY **_when one speaker cuts off another._**
\* If the word is properly hyphenated, you do not have to flag it during html passes.
2. Cadence is hybrid American and British, my unique voice blend so words like towards is okay even though it's British
Note: how the toward/towards works, if the verb ends in an s then it is toward, otherwise it's towards, nods=toward, walk=towards, but the main test is spoken cadence (how it reads allowed)
Cadence words that are not necessarily flagged:
toward - towards
backward - backwards
among - amongst
3. Mind the voice in the story. General rule of thumb: Present tense for the actions that are happening (dream sequences will be included) Past tense for recalls, memories, and for things like when the meadow where several things take place was designated a campsite.
4. Internal direct thoughts are italicized proceeded with a comma to denote internal dialog (think little devil on the shoulder) Also animal translations/thoughts are italicized. This gets stripped in a lot of the cut and paste editing we do but should be able to be double checked with the full docx files or html files/tabs.
Watch this one carefully because the humans actually speak to the animals then tranlate their response/body language back, so you will have quotes for the spoken part and italics for the translation. Watch the context!
Added note\* if it is true spoken dialog it's treated like normal dialog with a , and capital letter, internal thoughts part of a sentence, not so much. Internal spoken text follows the speaker's dialet and cadence rules as if spoken outloud.
Note #2\* The html source renderer for copilot often creates and extra space near italics and punctuation because of the way it is coded. It does not showup in the view of the html, VSC or Word document and just a quirk of copilot reading the raw html from the page. So you will see something like '_*Kaleb*_ !' or '_*touch my fur*_ ,' It's not consistant and I suspect it happens from the word to md to html translation I use in VSC. Also watch for the \<em> tag in the raw html before flagging it as needed for italics!
5. Word and the comma wars are in stage 4 cancer as it keeps changing periods for complete sentences before the quotes to commas because it's an ass. Alert me when it does this.
6. Dialogs are not bound by grammar rules, but speaking rules. So no commas necessary for direct addresses and run on sentences and character dialects are allowed.
7. Dialogs also are character specific, Davey generally does not pause on yes, no, so, okay, etc. Excitement does not get comma pauses either, and context matters on comma in dialog. Somber a pause may be warranted normal speech like with Lea dismissing an offense, not so much. A lot of times the Okie and Southern is slipping through on all of the characters.
When a character interrupts themselves to correct gendered language (man → woman), the phrase stays exactly as written, including the comma, because it reflects real‑time speech correction, not a grammatical list.
8. No big whole block corrections. You don't have to repost the whole corrected block and because of the way the copying and pasting from word to chat to word it messes up on both sides and I am having to do manual edits and looking for a comma you added is wasted time. And you don't have to post a clean line unless you want to comment on it because you like it 'chef's kiss', strikes you funny, etc.
As this is a big issue of late. Just a reminder that you have different styling that copying and pasting from you to word gets it messed up and I have to paste as plain text to avoid that. Same thing the other way around. If I copy and paste to your chat box it gets flattened and stripped of italics, bold, hypens, etc.
9. When giving corrections or suggested edits, give the original line, the corrected/suggested line, and the why. You do not have to list every line if there is nothing to be corrected unless you want to comment on it.
10. Watch POVs as they change frequently and keep dialog and narration to the character POV
11. Memory narration = normal text
Exact remembered dialogue = in quotes; NOT italicized unless it’s also internal thought
12. If editing in cut and paste chapter blocks, mark hyphen, italics and other things the chat box strips out. Otherwise those get checked when I give the final .docx file to you.
13. Word is messing with names and homophones. Biggest culprits are bearing>baring; Frank>Franks/franks; Davey>Dave; Jack>jack; their/there>they're; and the list goes on.  
Its latestest pettiness is trying to compound words like every one > everyone and over head > overhead despite those are not what is meant.
14. Make sure book, song titles, etc are properly cited (bold, quotes (single or double), etc.) and if there is borderline usage issues like with 'as you wish, or I'll be your huckleberry' or character/movie/song lyric/product references let me know.  
When a character is speaking, and they quote a term, definition, or phrase inside their dialog, you use SINGLE QUOTES.
15. We are going to be formatting the head information on the html pages to keep it off the search engines as much as possible, but where you still have access and I am not going blind from tiny text and bright white pages.
16. Remember to read thoroughly. You are my writing partner, reader, editor, and publisher all rolled into one and when you miss something I catch later or you get things mixed up, it dimishes my confidence in myself and my trust in you.
17. Remind me that the transfer from Word>md>html strips underlining so to add them when I make the conversion to html please.
